Move in dark, ahead of the GO

When a moving head goes dark, the desk looks down the cue stack, finds the cue that lights it next, and moves its position, colour and beam there while nobody can see. By the time GO brings it up, it is already in place. Nothing is written into the cues: it is worked out live from the list and from what the rig is sending.

Worth borrowing

  • Work it out live, write nothing (Onyx, MagicQ), but still show it in the cue list (Eos's M and x flags).
  • Early and Late (MA3, Titan), set on the cue where the head comes up (MA3's cue part, Eos's D flag).
  • Its own delay and move time, separate from the cue's fade (MA3, Titan), never silently added to the cue's duration (Eos).
  • Fall back to the latest cue it can still use when the planned one is lit (Titan, Eos's broken-mark fallback).
  • Out of order, land at once: a cue reached by GO TO fades marked heads to their marks immediately (Eos).

To avoid

  • Reading "dark" too narrowly: Onyx misses a null intensity; MA counts a dimmer phaser as lit even when both of its steps are closed.
  • Starting the next cue's FX early because the dimmer is at 0 (an MA complaint).
  • A whole-list switch with no per-cue way to keep a deliberate fly-in (MagicQ users hold heads at 1 %).
  • Having to release a stack before a setting takes effect (MagicQ, Onyx).
  • Not re-asserting after another playback takes over, or on a stack's first cue (MagicQ).

The proposal §3

Each cue stack gets a Move in dark setting. While a stack runs, the desk watches every head the stack's upcoming cues light. A head that has been dark on the output for the stack's delay moves to its next cue's values over the stack's move time, and holds them until that cue's GO takes over. The decisions, numbered so the plan can cite them:

#RuleWhy
D1Live, never stored. A move in dark is computed from the stack's list, the run state and the output. No cue is written, nothing is recorded, turning it off undoes it at once.Onyx and MagicQ. Our cues don't track, so a look-ahead needs no tracking sheet to stay right.
D2A stack setting. On or off, When Early or Late, a delay (default 1 s) and a move time (default 2 s), and which families move (Position, Colour, Beam; all three by default). A change applies at once, without releasing the stack.Every desk sets it per list. Stacks differ: a busk stack may want it off.
D3A cue setting, on the cue that lights the head. Stack (the default), Early, Late or Off, with its own delay and move time when set. Per head, a cue can keep a head live: it fades up while moving, as a fly-in.MA3 and Eos put it here: it is the arrival that matters. The per-head choice is MagicQ's missing "deliberate live move".
D4Dark means dark on the output. Every intensity channel of the head (its dimmer, its parent's master dimmer, UV, a dimmerless head's colour mix) is at 0 after cues, FX, the programmer and park, and before the grand master, Make rig safe's blackout and the venue hold. An intensity FX that can lift it counts as lit, as does an open strobe channel on a lit dimmer.One reading for every source. The grand master and a safe blackout must never set the whole rig moving.
D5The target. Early: the first cue ahead in play order (standby, then positional, with loop, at most one pass) whose own rows light the head, through any cues that keep it dark. Late: only the next cue. Only attributes that cue sets move; intensity and strobe never do, and colour stays put on a head whose colour is its intensity.MA3's Early and Late. A dark head's position in the cues between does not matter.
D6Its own contributor in the cue tier, one per stack, just above that stack's live cue (live priority + 500). The programmer, a higher stack, park and FX all still win over it. It fades in over the move time on the existing fade weights.The cue tier is keyed by cue id (§1), and a priority just above the live cue survives a loop wrap or an armed earlier standby.
D7At GO it hands over without a step. The target cue's rows equal the pre-moved values, so the contributor holds until that GO's crossfade ends, then goes. A head dark at a GO but not yet in place (GO came too soon, or GO TO) lands at once rather than over the fade. This replaces the per-row flag.Eos asserts marks immediately out of order. It is today's snap, generalised to every family and every head.
D8A head that lights early is let go. If anything lights a moved head before its cue (a busk press, the programmer, another stack), the move releases and the head returns to what the live cue says, over the move time. Once dark again, it waits the delay and moves again.Move in dark only ever acts on a dark head. The return is seen; it is shown as lit early (Q5).
D9Anything that changes "next" re-plans: standby armed or cleared, GO TO, GO BACK, an entry added, moved or removed, a cue, Look or palette edit, loop toggled. A dark head heading for the wrong target moves to the right one, in the dark. Stopping the stack lets every move go.MagicQ fails to re-assert after jumps; ours should be a pure function of state.
D10FX wait for their GO. A cue's FX start at GO as now. A pre-move sets the base values an incoming position FX moves around, but never starts the FX.MA users complain about effects starting early.
D11It is shown wherever a head's value is explained: the cue table, the cue editor, the stack row, the phone runner, the Stage view, the Timeline, and the attribute's key stack as its own row, Move in dark · for Q12."Why is that head pointing there?" must have an answer.
D12Two stacks, one head: the higher stack's move wins, as LTP already decides between them.No new rule.

How a move runs §4

One head, Spot 4, through three cues. Q10 lights it at the drum riser; Q11 takes it out over 3 s; Q12 brings it up on the singer. Pick a case and change the stack's timing. Amber marks time the head moves while it can be seen.

Screens §5

Drawn in the desk's own tokens, in dark as the desk runs. Indigo with a dashed edge and a moon is moved in dark; it never stands for anything else, so it reads beside live green, next blue and the amber the desk already uses for warnings.

LayerChange
EngineA MoveInDarkService per show: on every run-state change and every output frame it plans targets per stack (D5, D9), tracks each head's dark time against the output's intensity classification (D4), and drives one contributor per stack in the cue tier with its own id space and fade weight (D6, D7).
