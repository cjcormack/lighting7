Applying a template to a value

A template is a named value for one attribute family. It has no order, no amount, no timing and no targets of its own — it is applied to a selection, and there are two gestures because there are two things you might mean.

The strip follows the selection

Colour Amber Key Steel Blue Warm White Lavender New from selection

Select colour cells and only colour templates are offered — the family of the selection is the filter, so there is no picker to open and no family dropdown to get wrong. The last chip records what you have selected as a new template, which is how the library fills up without anyone visiting it.

Not every template is one value

A focus position is the counter-example, and it is one of the most useful things on a desk: eight heads aimed at the same spot on stage hold eight different pan/tilt pairs. So a template is generic (one value, any head) or per fixture (recorded from where the heads actually are) — the row says which.

This is the existing deferred/bound row split, kept as an internal detail rather than promoted to two library sections. Applying a per-fixture template to a head it has no entry for simply asserts nothing for that head, and the strip greys it.
