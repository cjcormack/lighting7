Design proposal · FX editor · 2026-10-26

FX settings, simplified without losing the engine

Every FX setting asks one of three questions: what the light does, how it travels across the heads, and how it sits over what's underneath. Today the editor mixes those up. Spread is a percentage of a cycle that has to be worked out by hand, three bare number boxes do the grouping, and 29 FX types repeat each other with different names for the same knob. This page proposes an editor built around the three questions, with the step-aligned chase you asked about as the default.

The case that started this

Four fixtures, three colours, Spread 100 %

Spread 100 % shares the cycle evenly between the heads, so four fixtures start a quarter of a cycle apart. Colour Cycle changes colour every third of a cycle. A quarter never lands on a third, so the colour edges stagger. The graph was right. At 133 % each fixture starts exactly one colour behind the last, but nobody should have to work out 4 ÷ 3 at a desk. MagicQ has the same arithmetic and leaves it to the operator. grandMA3 2.4 added Adaptive Width for two-step phasers only. Onyx offers Step per X beside Wave per X, which is the idea taken here.

1

Timing is one strip. Cycle, master, direction and runs sit on one row of chips. A chip opens its choices: Cycle's eight steps and a typed beats field, the masters and Seconds, Runs' ×1 to ×4 and a count. One-shots come out of Advanced because they are a busking move.

2

The FX's own settings, named for what they hold. Here Colours; a Wave's section is Levels, a Path's Path, a step FX's Steps. Never Look, which is the library's word for a recorded state. The header's FX name is the type switcher. Colour Cycle absorbs Colour Fade and Colour Wave on the desk: a list of colours, and Between colours: Cut · Fade · Smooth (Fade has a %). From palettes… switches to Palette Steps and taps palettes in order, as MagicQ's 2col / 3col does.

3

Across the heads leads with what will happen. The ladder is the selection's own heads, each numbered by when it starts, recomputed by the desk's preview as in the graph. Pattern chips set the order and grouping together, and keep today's drawings, now drawn on the selection's own heads (four dots here) rather than eight notional ones. Each head later by says how far apart neighbours start, in the FX's own unit: 1 step is the default for a step FX and holds when the selection changes. Fit the cycle is today's 100 %, and Free opens a slider in steps or cycles. Segments, Parts and Wings keep MagicQ's words, but each becomes a segment with … for a typed count, and one line under them says what the current values do, in the heads' own terms. The same rule holds everywhere: a segment of presets always ends in a typed value, so a preset never limits what can be set.

4

Mix is how much and how. Amount moves here beside the family's question (Replace it · Within it for a level, Around · Absolute for a path). A colour FX has no question, so the readout says what the blend does. The raw blend modes go to Advanced, as before.

5

Advanced keeps the rest: phase, One cycle for: all heads · each head (today's Timing per cycle / per head), the head filter, the raw blend and start on the beat. Its summary line lists anything not at its default, so nothing changed here is hidden.

FX types

29 built-ins become 12, grouped into 9 picker entries

Many built-ins are the same maths under another name: Pulse, Iris Pulse and Frost Pulse share one script; Sine Wave and Zoom Wave another; Ramp Down is Ramp Up read backwards. On the desk they merge into Wave, Colour Cycle and Path, each with a Shape or a Between choice. A one-shot data step rewrites every stored FX onto the new types once, and LegacyNames maps old ids and parameters on import, in MCP and in a script's effect("SineWave"), so nothing stored or scripted breaks. A migration never changes what the rig does: a stored FX with no exact equivalent stays on its old type, kept registered and hidden from the picker. The Flicker family and the step FX keep their own types, since their timing models differ, and the picker groups them. Labels such as onValue, offLevel and maxBrightness become Low · High.

Decided · 2026-10-26

Seven answers

  1. Consolidate the types on the desk

    Not looks over unchanged types. Wave, Colour Cycle and Path replace the built-ins that share their maths; the Flicker family and the step FX stay separate types under one picker entry each, because their timing models differ.

  2. Rewrite stored FX once, and read old names

    A one-shot data step (the next user_version) rewrites every Look, cue and palette FX onto the new types, and LegacyNames maps old ids and parameters wherever an old name can still arrive: an archive, MCP, a script's effect(). An FX with no exact equivalent stays on its old type, hidden from the picker.

  3. "1 step" is stored

    A synced spreadUnit the desk resolves at expansion, so a fixture joining the selection keeps the chase aligned.

  4. A new step FX starts at 1 step

    Colour Cycle, Palette Steps and Slot Steps started from any door begin at 1 step; a stored FX naming no unit keeps CYCLE.

  5. Keep MagicQ's words

    Segments · Parts · Wings stay on screen and in the glossary, each a segment with a typed count and a line saying what the current values do.

  6. Width: One head in the same plan

    A Wave parameter, read against the slot count the FX already knows.

  7. Cycle as a chip

    In the inspector's Timing strip; the graph's Cycle tab stays a drag.
