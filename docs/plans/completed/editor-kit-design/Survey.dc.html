Today: two answers to each of three questions

The busk view shipped a Spread tab, a Colour tab and a pair of numeric endpoint editors between 2026-09-17 and 2026-09-22, and each is the better design. The programmer's Fan popover, colour popover and four cell editors predate them. This board is the survey: what each side draws today, and the differences that matter. The three boards after it draw the one answer; Model says where the code goes and what is open.

Same question, two panels. Both take a selection and two ends and put a value on every head between them. The left one asks the browser to interpolate bytes over the rows it can see and lands them through the cell writers; the right one asks the desk to interpolate an intent and lands what the desk answers. The right one is the one the template system, the Look press and every other write on this desk already agree with.

What the programmer's has that the busk tab does not: the column chooser when the marquee spans two fannable columns (a select), Reverse, and the focused-Look-layer arm — a spread there lands in the layer's row draft. All three survive in the proposal; the first two as the family segment and Order: Reverse, the third as one flag on the route.

The body is already shared — ColourPickerBody was extracted for the busk tab in session 5. What is not shared is everything around it: the read-out, Pick, Recent, the footer, and the count line. The tab grew them; the popover did not get them back.

The popover is the older layout of the same body: a 200px square pinned in index.css, a guidance paragraph under it, emitter rows with no counts, and a count line at the top saying what row C one line up already says. The tab's version is fluid, counts its emitters per head, reads the selection back with Pick, and keeps its verbs in a footer.

Five things differ, none of them a design. The label style (11px sentence vs 9px uppercase); the count line (Applying to 4 targets at the top vs nothing, the band carrying it); the field height (32 in the level editor, 28 in every other); the unit (bytes in the level editor where the cell reads 80%; bytes in the position editor where the busk view types degrees); and the popover width (256 for three editors, w-auto for the colour one, 224 for settings).

The busk view has no value editor of its own beyond the two endpoint fields, and it should not grow one — but its endpoint fields are the anatomy the cell editors should take: a label over a field with the unit inside it, at 28px.

What already is one thing, and stays: CellEditorSurface's three forms, useCellEditorKeyboard (Enter applies, comma steps, the first field focused), useNumberFieldDraft, ValueFieldRow, UnsetCellMark. The kit is real; it stops one component short of the busk view.

The rules the next three boards apply
One name: Spread

The verb on row C, the panel, the plan kinds and the module. "Fan" survives only in the desk survey as what other desks call it.

One panel per question

A spread is one panel whatever hosts it: the programmer's popover, the busk tab, the phone sheet. The colour editor is one body with one read-out and one footer. Hosts choose a form and fill slots; they do not redraw.

The desk resolves

Every spread over a property in the template vocabulary goes through POST /programmer/spread. The client lerps only what the desk has no grammar for: addresses and fade times, and raw bytes on a column outside the vocabulary.

Drawn once on a screen

Recent chips are on row C's strip in the programmer and in the sheet on the busk view; the same screen never shows them twice. The count is on row C or the band, so no editor repeats it as a top line — the read-out under a picker says what the count line cannot (which heads take which emitter).

The anatomy is the busk tab's

A 9px uppercase label over a control; 28px fields with the unit inside; segmented rows for a choice of four or fewer; a picker padded to its knob; a read-out line; a static footer with the save first and the verbs last.

Live where a value is judged by eye

Cell editors already write as they go. Spread gains Live on both sides, off by default; Apply always sends; Send again while Live is on.
