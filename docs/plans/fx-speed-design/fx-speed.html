Design · FX speed · agreed 2026-11-06

FX speed, set by ear

An FX's speed is a cycle length in beats and bars today. It follows a speed master or runs on Seconds, and a cue sets a tempo from a list typed into its Cue tab. This page proposes three changes. Speed becomes a slider against the master, snapping from ×¼ to ×4. Own tempo, set by tapping, replaces Seconds. Tempo goes into the programmer, so Record puts it into the cue.

  • A master relates to an FX by scaling it. grandMA3 has Speed Scale, Hog and Titan have rate percentages, Hog's batch faders run 0× to 4×. The proposal's ×-slider is that idea on the FX itself.
  • Tap is the main way to set a tempo, and an FX's own speed can be tapped too (Eos's Learn Time, MagicQ's Tap to time).
  • The speed a cue plays is recorded from the live state. Eos stars the change once it is recorded. No desk makes you type a tempo into a cue's list.
The model

Tempo times Speed gives the cycle

An FX runs against a tempo: a speed master's, or its own. Its Speed says how fast it goes against that tempo. At ×1 it makes one cycle a bar, and every snap doubles or halves it. The cycle in beats stays what the desk stores (Speed is 4 ÷ cycle), so no beat FX changes when this lands. The readout gives the cycle in seconds at the tempo it is running at.

Try it

The Speed control, live

A Wave on one head, drawn over the last two bars, with the beat grid. Tap a few times to set the tempo. Drag Speed: it sticks at the snaps unless Snap is off. The phase never jumps. Off a snap the FX drifts off the beat grid, and when it lands on a snap it glides back onto the grid within a cycle. Taps work the way the desk counts them: the last four taps, any older than two seconds dropped.

  • 1 · Tempo chip. The masters with their family badges, then Own tempo, then Same speed as… (today's Same cycle as…). A new FX is stamped with its family's master, as now.
  • 2 · Tap. On a master it taps that master's tempo into the programmer, so every FX on it follows, the rail shows it held, and Record stores it. On Own tempo it sets the FX's own. Two to four taps, as the desk counts.
  • 3 · The tempo, typed or tapped. Amber while the programmer holds it.
  • 4 · The slider with its five labelled snaps (the drawing shows them as a segment), its outer ticks at ×⅛, ×1/16, ×8 and ×16, and a typed value at its end that takes a fraction (×⅔) or a decimal. It sticks at every power of two. Hold ⌥ to slide past a snap.
  • 5 · The readout, in bars or beats where the speed is a snap, and always in seconds at the running tempo.
  • 6 · Direction and Runs, unchanged.

Tempo in the programmer

Tap it while you build the cue, then Record

The programmer holds tempos the way it holds scenery. A tap or a typed tempo in the FX editor holds that master's tempo, and so does Capture on a rail card, which captures a busked tempo. A held master runs at its held tempo at once. Record writes the held tempos into the cue's Tempo on GO. The rail and the surfaces stay live otherwise: a tap there is still beaten by the next cue that sets that master.

The Cue tab's list stays, renamed Tempo on GO. It is where a recorded tempo shows and where one can still be fixed by hand. Record is now the usual way in.

Extras

Half time, double time, and a speed fader

×½ and ×2 on every master

On each rail card, in the master sheet, and as a surface button target (speedMasterScale, factor ½ or 2). Each halves or doubles the tempo, as a tempo write: live, or moving the hold on a held master. A button is disabled where the result would leave 20–300 BPM, and on a follower, whose leader's buttons do it. A tap afterwards sets the beat it hears, as grandMA3's Learn does by default, and doesn't keep the factor.

FX speed on a fader or encoder

A binding target, fxSpeed, that moves the Speed of every Local FX painting a selected head. An encoder moves each FX a quarter of an octave a detent, each sticking at its own snaps. A fader sets them all from ×¼ to ×4 with soft takeover against the lead FX, the one painting the first selected head. The ring shows the lead's Speed. The phase is kept, as in the editor. This is what Hog and Onyx operators use a speed fader for.

Out of scope

  • Speed counted in steps, so a longer colour list runs slower (FU-FX-PER-STEP-TIMING stays dormant).
  • MIDI clock, Ableton Link and audio beat detection.
  • One FX at two speeds on two attributes (FU-SPEED-PER-ATTRIBUTE).
  • A tap on a surface confirmed by its LED (FU-SPEED-SURFACE-TAP-LED).

Also agreed

  • Tempo is the screen word for a master's rate, freeing Speed for the FX. The glossary gave Speed to the master.
  • A tap from the rail or a surface on a held master moves the hold, rather than going live underneath where nothing would show it.
Asked and answered

Six answers

  1. What does ×1 mean?

    One cycle a bar, today's default, so a new beat FX at its default reads ×1 (a preset's own cycle reads as it is, the sweeps ×2). One cycle a beat (Hog's reading) and a step FX counting steps were declined.

  2. What replaces Seconds?

    Own tempo, tapped, with the same slider on it. Masters only, and keeping Seconds with a Tap, were declined.

  3. Which taps go into the programmer?

    The FX editor's, plus Capture on a rail card (first drawn as Hold, renamed because the glossary's Hold is a Pulse's). The rail and the surfaces stay live. Every tap being held was declined, because a busk tap would then block the next cue's tempo.

  4. Extras?

    Both: ×½ and ×2 on masters, and an FX speed fader or encoder.

  5. A master's word?

    Tempo, so Speed is the FX's.

  6. A rail or surface tap on a held master?

    It moves the hold.
