Scrims and painted cloths in the Stage view

A pantomime is built from cloths: painted backcloths and frontcloths, cut cloths and foliage borders, a gauze for the transformation, and a day cloth that turns to night when it is lit from behind. Today the Stage view draws every drape as plain pleated velour that stops every beam. This record shows what the view would look like with those cloths modelled properly, and what the desk needs to get there.

4fabrics: canvas, muslin, sharkstooth, bobbinet
2params keys: fabric and paint
1format bump (v23, for the image folder)
6sessions, each building on the last

What the pictures are. Every image on this page is drawn live by a small ray tracer in the page. It applies the proposed rules (§"Decisions proposed") to a model stage: a proscenium, a gauze just upstage of it, a room set, a foliage cut cloth that can be flown in, and a translucent sky cloth painted for day on its front and night on its back. The paintings are procedural stand-ins for the uploaded images the desk would use. It is a sketch of the model, not the desk's shader: no gobos, no lens optics and simpler falloff. The numbers that decide how much light passes are marked estimate, as the stage-light plan marks its finishes.

Three ways to hang a scrim

A scrim is still a drape, and it moves the way the drape's existing Moves by setting says. Flown, it travels on its trimM. On a track, it draws on its open state. Dead-hung, it stays put. The cloth's own rules stay the same in every case. One thing does change on a track: as a drawn scrim gathers, its folds stack layers of net, and the gathered part goes nearly solid.

Flown

A gauze that flies out once the scene behind is lit. The usual transformation reveal. It carries trimM and a travel time, like any flown drape.

The look

Fabric, colour and seat

The same bleed-through state under different cloths and from different seats. Bobbinet is a finer, more open net than sharkstooth, so it never quite reads as solid. A black scrim barely catches the wash, so the room shows through it at almost any level. From a side seat, the holes close up and the same gauze looks denser. That is real, and it is why a reveal that works in the centre stalls can fail at the ends of a row. Seat views will say so (D13).

The look

Painted cloths

Any drape or flat can carry a painted image. A transparent area in the image is a hole: a cut cloth's arch, the gaps between a foliage border's leaves, the window of a cut-out flat. Light, haze and the eye pass straight through it, and the cloth's shadow follows the cut edge. A translucent muslin cloth takes a second image for its back, which is how a day cloth turns to night.

Day and night on one cloth

The front is painted in translucent dyes for day. The back is painted opaque wherever the night should be dark, leaving the moon, the stars and the windows open. Front light shows the front paint. Light from behind shines through both layers, so it shows through only where the back is open, coloured by the dye on the front. That is why the windows glow amber and the sky reads blue.

Cut cloths and borders

The image's alpha channel cuts the cloth: below half opacity is a hole. The same mask drives the surface (a hole draws what is behind it), the shadows (the occlusion test samples the mask where a segment crosses the cloth), beam reach and the haze. So a back light through a foliage cut cloth throws shafts through the leaves, as the fourth still shows.

The model

Why the angle matters

A scrim's threads are round, and at an angle they hide the holes between them. Below, a section through one set of threads is drawn to scale, with the thread a third of the pitch (sharkstooth's estimate). Parallel rays come in at three angles from the cloth's normal. The ones that pass are drawn in amber. The ones that hit a thread stop there.

One function covers both what a beam passes and what an eye sees through. The cross threads take their own share whatever the tilt:

open(θ) = (1 − r) · max(0, 1 − r ⁄ cos θ)
r = thread ⁄ pitch · sharkstooth r = 0.30 · bobbinet r = 0.15

The markers are this page's rig and seats, measured against the gauze's normal. The steep wash comes in at 86° and passes nothing: all of it lands on the threads. That is why designers light scrims from straight above or below. A flat FOH front light at 19° passes nearly half its light on to the set.

The model

Light through the cloth

A section along the centre line, to scale in metres, with the same rig as the live stage. Today every collider stops a beam outright (beamReach.ts) and shadows everything behind it (occlusion.ts). Scrims and cut cloths need a third answer: the cloth passes a share, so a beam continues to the next solid surface at the strength left to it. For a scrim that share is open(θ). For a cut cloth it is the image's mask at the point crossed.

  • Steep wash (amber, from above the gauze): it meets the threads at 86° and passes nothing, so the floor just upstage of the gauze stays dark.
  • Flat FOH (amber, from the house): 48 % carries on, dashed, and lands on the set. That is the mistake the Flat FOH preset shows.
  • The eye (blue): from the centre stalls it sees 49 % of what is behind. The back light's haze, shaded upstage of the gauze, reaches the house at that share too.
The model

One pixel through a cloth

A scrim draws as a blend over what is behind it, weighted by how much of the cloth the eye sees at that angle. The haze march splits at the cloth's plane, so haze in front of the gauze is not dimmed and haze behind it is. A cut cloth is the same blend with the mask in place of the angle, so it is either cloth or a hole. Muslin is opaque to the eye and to beams, but its front glows with light from behind.

scrim  pixel = haze in front + (1 − open(θeye)) · threads + open(θeye) · (surface behind + haze behind)
muslin front = paintfront · Efront + τ · paintfront ⊙ paintback · Ebehind   τ = 0.45, estimate

The desk

In the scene document

A cloth's fabric and paint are separate choices from its role (a default colour and finish) and its operation (how it moves). If scrim were a role, "scrim tabs" could not be said, and a painted leg would need a role of its own.

  • DrapeParams.fabric: CANVAS | MUSLIN | SHARKSTOOTH | BOBBINET, absent meaning velour as today. Canvas is a flat, opaque, matte painted cloth. Muslin is flat and translucent. The two nets are scrims.
  • paint: { front?, back? } on DrapeParams and FlatParams, each the SHA-256 of a stored image. On a flat, the front is its downstage face and its openings still cut through. back is the upstage face, and on muslin it is the night side.
  • Every fabric but velour hangs flat, so depthM pleats only velour, and a painted velour folds its paint with its pleats. A drawn cloth folds as it gathers.
  • The image stretches over the element's width and height. The sheet warns when the two aspects differ by more than 2 % and offers Match height to image.
  • A scrim's or muslin's default colour is off-white, unless finishColour says otherwise, the way a cyc's is. Under paint, the finish colour is the unpainted back.
  • The MCP vocabulary in SetupToolSchemas.kt gains both keys, so set_scene can build one: DRAPE: {role, operation, fabric, paint, travelS}.

The desk

Images on the desk

Painted images are the second binary the desk syncs, after the prompt-book PDF. They follow the PDF's contract: content-addressed, outside the text machinery, and copied by raw bytes.

WhereWhat
Stored<appDataDir>/scene-images/{projectUuid}/{sha256}.{png|jpg}, the original as uploaded (PNG or JPEG, which the JVM decodes with no new dependency; WebP is a follow-up; at most 8192 px on its longest side and 25 MB). No table: params name the hash, as a prompt book names its PDF.
DerivedA display copy at 2048 px with mipmaps for the Stage view, and a 256 px alpha mask for the occlusion atlas and beam reach. Both are made once, cached per machine and never synced.
SyncedsceneImages/{sha256}.{ext} in the show repo, only the hashes some element references. formatVersion 23, because an older reader's wipe-then-export push would delete the folder for every peer (v4's reason). minReader stays 5. .gitattributes marks the folder binary, and walkTree and the UUID remapper skip it.
UploadedPOST /api/rest/projects/{id}/scene-images with the raw bytes answers {hash, width, height, hasAlpha}. GET …/scene-images/{hash} serves the original, and ?variant=display | detail | mask a derived copy. A paint hash that names no stored image is refused at the write boundary.
MCPupload_scene_image {mediaType, base64} stores an image and answers its hash, for set_scene to name. Capped at the MCP message size. Stored data only, so it carries no remote-access gate, as set_scene carries none.
ClonedExport → import, as everything is. The importer hydrates sceneImages/ into the new project's store, as it hydrates promptScripts/.

The texture budget is why the display copy exists. Twenty painted cloths at 2048 px are about 220 MB of GPU memory with mipmaps. A hero frontcloth can load at 4096 px instead: a per-element Full detail switch, kept per machine. At 8192 px they would be about 3.5 GB.

The desk

Decisions proposed

#Decision
D1Fabric is a cloth's, not a role. fabric: CANVAS | MUSLIN | SHARKSTOOTH | BOBBINET on DrapeParams, absent = velour. Role and operation are unchanged, and every use is a combination of the three.
D2Only velour pleats. Every other fabric hangs flat. Paint follows velour’s pleats. A drawn cloth folds as it gathers, with an amplitude that grows with the gathering.
D3One function of angle for a scrim. open(θ) = (1 − r)·max(0, 1 − r/cos θ) decides both what a beam passes and what an eye sees through. Sharkstooth r = 0.30, bobbinet r = 0.15 estimate. Gathered net stacks: openc.
D4Drapes and flats take paint. paint: {front?, back?}, each a stored image's SHA-256, stretched over the element's face. The images are a content-addressed binary store synced at sceneImages/ (v23).
D5Alpha cuts. An image pixel below half opacity is a hole, in the surface, the shadows, beam reach and the haze alike, through a 256 px mask per image.
D6Muslin is translucent. It is opaque to beams and to the eye. Light from behind lights its front through τ · paintfront ⊙ paintback, with τ = 0.45 estimate, so a back painting shows only where it is left open.
D7A part may transmit. Besides colliding or not, a part may pass a share of light: by angle (a scrim) or by mask (a cut cloth). Beam reach and the landing planes skip a transmitting part, so a beam carries on to the next solid surface, and Focus here still takes the point it is given.
D8Shadows become transmittance. The box occlusion test multiplies by the crossing's share for each transmitting collider and carries on, instead of returning blocked. The packed collider gains a kind (a third texel), and masks sit in an atlas of at most 32. The landing-plane fallback does the same.
D9Seeing through is a blend. A scrim draws without writing depth, premultiplied over what is behind it, weighted by 1 − open(θeye). Its threads wrap grazing light and glow faintly from behind estimate. A cut cloth discards its holes and writes depth elsewhere, so it stays opaque cloth with holes in it.
D10Haze splits at the plane. Each beam carries the transmitting planes on its path. Samples past them scale by the beam's crossing, and samples behind one from the eye by the eye's, from one list of eight shared by scrims and cut cloths, nearest the eye first. A ninth still passes light on surfaces, but its haze does not split.
D11Uploads from the desk and from MCP. A file picker on the element sheet, a REST upload, and upload_scene_image for Claude. No fetch by URL.
D12The GPU sees a 2048 px copy. The original is stored and synced. The view loads a derived display copy with mipmaps, and occlusion reads the 256 px mask. A per-element Full detail switch loads 4096 px for a hero cloth, kept per machine in machine_overrides, never synced.
D13Seat views flag a solid-looking scrim. When a seat sees a scrim at below half its head-on open, its popover and the viewpoint picker say so, e.g. "Forest gauze reads near-solid from F6 (open 12 %)".
D14Out of scope: moiré, a visible weave, projection mapping, painted surfaces other than drapes and flats, and bounce light (FU-STAGE-BOUNCE-FILL).
The desk

Proposed order

Six sessions, one PR each. The order is chosen for clean development rather than for which feature lands first. Session 1 settles the document and the store once, so no later session touches sync. Session 3 builds one transmittance core that scrims and cut-outs both use, so the light model is written once. Sessions 4 and 5 then teach the surface shader and the haze about it. Both features are complete after session 5.

SessionWhat landsSize
1The document and the image store (backend-heavy). fabric and paint in Kotlin params and validation. The image store, its REST routes and the derived copies. Sync at v23, with the exporter, importer, .gitattributes, walkTree and remapper skips, and RichProjectFixture carrying a real image. upload_scene_image and the set_scene vocabulary. The element sheet's Fabric select and image pickers, and elementProblems.ts. Nothing new is drawn yet (D1, D4, D11, D12).large
2Painted surfaces. Flat cloth for every fabric but velour. Front and back images on drapes and flats, with the image continuous across a drawn cloth's halves as they gather. Display copies loaded with mipmaps (D2, D4, D12).medium
3The transmittance core. The transmitting part, by angle or by mask. The collider kind and the mask atlas in occlusion, the CPU mask in beam reach, and the landing fallback. After this, light passes scrims and cut-outs and shadows follow the cut edge (D3, D5, D7, D8).medium
4See-through surfaces. The scrim blend with its wrap and glow, cut-out discard, and muslin's translucency with back paint. The GLSL for open(θ) gets a TypeScript twin pinned by a test (D6, D9).medium
5Haze. Transmitting planes in the march, by angle and by mask, one shared list of eight (D10). Measured in ?profileHarness on the three machines, with the haze governor absorbing the cost.large
6Tuning and the seat hint. ?profileHarness=scrim, =cutcloth and =daynight, to judge r, the wrap, the glow and τ by eye. The seat-view hint (D13).small
