A scrim in the Stage view

A scrim is cloth with holes in it. Lit steeply from the front, it reads as solid. Light what is behind it and take the front light out, and it disappears. Today the Stage view draws every drape as solid pleated cloth that stops every beam, so it cannot show that reveal. This record shows what the view would look like with a scrim modelled properly, whether it is flown, drawn on a track or dead-hung, and what the desk needs to get there.

Three ways to hang it

A scrim is still a drape, and it moves the way the drape's existing Moves by setting says. Flown, it travels on its trimM. On a track, it draws on its open state. Dead-hung, it stays put. The cloth's own rules stay the same in every case. One thing does change on a track: as a drawn scrim gathers, its folds stack layers of net, and the gathered part goes nearly solid.

Flown

A gauze that flies out once the scene behind is lit. The usual memory or dream reveal. It carries trimM and a travel time, like any flown drape.

The look

Fabric, colour and seat

The same bleed-through state under different cloths and from different seats. Bobbinet is a finer, more open net than sharkstooth, so it never quite reads as solid. A black scrim barely catches the wash, so the room shows through it at almost any level. From a side seat, the holes close up and the same gauze looks denser. That is real, and it is why a reveal that works in the centre stalls can fail at the ends of a row.

The model

Why the angle matters

A scrim's threads are round, and at an angle they hide the holes between them. Below, a section through one set of threads is drawn to scale, with the thread a third of the pitch (sharkstooth's estimate). Parallel rays come in at three angles from the cloth's normal. The ones that pass are drawn in amber. The ones that hit a thread stop there.

One function covers both what a beam passes and what an eye sees through. The cross threads take their own share whatever the tilt:

open(θ) = (1 − r) · max(0, 1 − r ⁄ cos θ)
r = thread ⁄ pitch · sharkstooth r = 0.30 · bobbinet r = 0.15

The markers are this page's rig and seats, measured against the gauze's normal. The steep wash comes in at 86° and passes nothing: all of it lands on the threads. That is why designers light scrims from straight above or below. A flat FOH front light at 19° passes nearly half its light on to the set.

The model

Light through the cloth

A section along the centre line, to scale in metres, with the same rig as the live stage. Today every collider stops a beam outright (beamReach.ts) and shadows everything behind it (occlusion.ts). A scrim needs a third answer: it passes a share, so a beam continues to the next solid surface, at the strength open(θ) leaves it.

  • Steep wash (amber, from above the gauze): it meets the threads at 86° and passes nothing, so the floor just upstage of the gauze stays dark.
  • Flat FOH (amber, from the house): 48 % carries on, dashed, and lands on the set. That is the mistake the Flat FOH preset shows.
  • The eye (blue): from the centre stalls it sees 49 % of what is behind. The back light's haze, shaded upstage of the gauze, reaches the house at that share too.
The model

One pixel through a scrim

The surface shader draws the scrim as a blend over what is behind it, weighted by how much of the cloth the eye sees at that angle. The haze march splits at the scrim plane, so the haze in front of the gauze is not dimmed and the haze behind it is.

pixel = haze in front + (1 − open(θeye)) · threads + open(θeye) · (surface behind + haze behind)

The desk

In the scene document

A scrim is a DRAPE with a fabric. Fabric is a separate choice from role, which only sets a default colour and finish, and from operation, which sets how the cloth moves. If scrim were a role, "scrim tabs" could not be said.

  • DrapeParams.fabric: SHARKSTOOTH | BOBBINET, absent meaning solid cloth as today. Written canonically, so absent stays absent.
  • A scrim hangs flat: depthM does not pleat it. A drawn one folds only as it gathers.
  • Its default colour is an off-white, unless finishColour says otherwise, the way a cyc's is.
  • The MCP vocabulary in SetupToolSchemas.kt gains the key, so set_scene can build one: DRAPE: {role, operation, fabric, travelS}.
  • No formatVersion bump: an optional params key, as travelS was (sync-engineering.md §"A params key added later"). An older desk keeps the key but refuses an edit to that element, the same caveat travelS carries.

The desk

Decisions proposed

#Decision
D1Fabric is a drape's, not a role. fabric: SHARKSTOOTH | BOBBINET on DrapeParams, absent = solid. Role and operation are unchanged, and every use is a combination of the three.
D2A scrim hangs flat. No pleat from depthM. A drawn scrim folds as it gathers, with an amplitude that grows with the gathering.
D3One function of angle. open(θ) = (1 − r)·max(0, 1 − r/cos θ) decides both what a beam passes and what an eye sees through. Sharkstooth r = 0.30, bobbinet r = 0.15 estimate. Gathered cloth stacks: openc.
D4A beam does not stop at a scrim. A part may be transmitting as well as colliding or not. Beam reach and the landing planes skip it, so the beam carries on to the next solid surface, and Focus here still takes the point it is given.
D5Shadows become transmittance. The box occlusion test multiplies by open(θ) for each scrim it crosses and carries on, instead of returning blocked. The packed collider gains a flag (a sign on the skin, or a third texel). The landing-plane fallback does the same.
D6Seeing through is a blend. The scrim draws without writing depth, premultiplied over what is behind it, weighted by 1 − open(θeye). Its threads wrap grazing light and glow faintly from behind estimate.
D7Haze splits at the plane. Each beam carries the first scrim on its axis as one more plane. Samples past it scale by open(θbeam), and samples behind a scrim from the eye by open(θeye), from a capped list of scrim rectangles, as regions are capped today.
D8No wire or sync change beyond the key. No formatVersion bump, no new state. The reveal is lighting, and the cloth's moves are the drape's existing trimM and open.
D9Out of scope: moiré, a visible weave, painted or projected images on the cloth (the scene document has no image finish), and bounce light (FU-STAGE-BOUNCE-FILL).
The desk

Proposed order

Four sessions, one PR each, in this order. After session 1 the light behaves correctly but the cloth still looks solid. Session 2 makes it look right from the house. Session 3 is the costly one and is measured against the frame budget.

SessionWhat landsSize
1The cloth and its shadows. fabric end to end (Kotlin params and validation, elementProblems.ts, the element form, the MCP vocabulary, RichProjectFixture, the docs). Flat geometry. The transmitting part state. Transmittance in beam reach, box occlusion and the landing fallback (D1, D2, D4, D5).medium
2Seeing through. The blend, open(θeye) and the threads' wrap and glow (D3, D6), with the GLSL chunk and its TypeScript twin pinned by a test.small
3Haze. Both attenuations in the march (D7). Measured in ?profileHarness on the three machines, with the haze governor absorbing the cost.large
4Tuning. A ?profileHarness=scrim scene, like this page's stage, to judge r, the wrap and the glow by eye against the classic reveal.small

Questions

  1. Bobbinet in the first cut? Proposed: yes. It is one more number for r, and a theatre that owns one will hang it.
  2. How many scrims may the haze honour at once? Proposed: four, as a uniform list like the regions. A fifth would still pass light on surfaces, but its haze would not split.
  3. Painted gauzes. The forest on this page is not in scope: the scene document has no image finish for any element. Is a painted scrim wanted enough to plan an image finish of its own?
  4. Seat check. Should the seat views warn when a scrim the seat sees through at a steep angle reads as solid there? That is the far-side frame in §"Fabric, colour and seat".
