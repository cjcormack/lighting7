How other desks say "one of these at a time"

Two camps: exclusivity as a property of the container the pads sit in, or as a playback rule on the record itself. The kinds of content that can share a container is the question the brief asks.

DeskLives onHow it works · what may share a group
QLC+containerThe Virtual Console's Solo Frame: only one child function runs at a time; a press releases the rest; separate solo frames don't affect each other. Any widget — scene, chaser, sequence, script — sits in one. Layout and function list are separate things.
ChamSys MagicQcontainerThe Execute window's Solo ("radio") buttons: one of the group active at a time, the group being every button in the Region, or the adjacent buttons when there is none. Cues and cue stacks share a region. The execute grid is its own layout, paged, apart from the cue-stack list.
Daslight 4bothA scene's Release mode — Off / General / Group / All except group — with the bank as the default group, so one scene per bank plays. Switches are a different kind that stacks. The kind decides the rule, which is the split the brief is trying not to make.
Lightkeylibrary groupA preset group has a "treat presets as mutually exclusive" option (4.2): adding one preset removes the group's others. Grouped cue buttons have their own grouping with an "always active" option. Closest to lighting7 today — and it keeps the two kinds apart, one grouping each.
grandMA3playback ruleNo group object. Kill on an executor turns it on and every other one off; Kill Protect opts a sequence out. Off when overridden switches a sequence off once another has taken every attribute it drove — contention inferred from channels, which is lighting7's per-target narrowing done automatically and for everyone.
Hog 4playback rulePer list or scene: Release on other Go, Persist on override, priority. Scenes (one-cue looks) and cue lists carry the same options; palettes carry none — they are data recalled into the programmer, not playbacks.
ETC EosnoneLTP and priority only. But its vocabulary is the point: palettes hold one category (intensity, focus, colour, beam) and presets hold all four with references to palettes — lighting7's template and Look — and direct selects put both on the same busk surface with no family wall between them.
Ableton / ResolumecontainerNot lighting, but the mental model busking operators arrive with: a track (or layer) is an exclusive lane, launching a clip stops the lane's other clip, and what a clip contains is the clip's business.

Container camp (QLC+, MagicQ, lanes): the layout is a separate object from the library, exclusivity is a flag on it, and content kinds mix freely. Price: the same record can sit in two containers with two behaviours, and the layout needs its own editing, paging and sync.

Rule camp (MA, Hog): no group at all; release follows from priority, override or a Kill press. Price: it cannot say "these two are alternatives" unless they overlap on channels, and it releases things the operator meant to stack — the Amber-under-Blue behaviour, but everywhere.

Where lighting7 sits. By the busking-view decision, the busk view is the library rendered as pads: there is one layout, and it is the library's order. So the two camps coincide here — a library group is the container. The pattern is not wrong; it is only typed too narrowly. Lightkey shows the library-group shape working; QLC+ and MagicQ show that nothing about a container needs to care what kind of thing a pad presses. What lighting7 has that none of them do is the per-target narrowing, and that is worth keeping across kinds.
