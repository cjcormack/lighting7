The page is the operator's, not the library's. Banks hold whatever they were given — templates, Looks and cues side by side — in the order they were placed. Movement and Key colours are solo: Ballyhoo was just pressed on the Movers, so its five siblings went dark on those heads. The other banks stack, the way ungrouped pads do today.

Rows, columns, banks. The page is two rows; the first row has three columns, the middle one stacking Key colours over Beam, and Cues is a bank whose pads run as a single column. Solo has nothing to do with shape. What left the page: The family columns, the Looks pool, the cue-stack cards and the pinned-cue grid. GO and BACK for the playhead stay on the ShowBar; a cue that wants a pad is placed in a bank like anything else and presses as a toggle, the way an FX cue slot does. A page is one screen at desk width, and more pages are a swipe or a tap away.
