FX editor

The FX on a selection, seen together and seen over time. The programmer's FX tab becomes a list of every FX on the selected heads, by family and in the order they apply; a graph of what each head and element will do; and the live editor beside them.

After the terminology plan 15 proposals 7 sessions 15 questions to answer

Every word on this page is the terminology plan's: FX, Cycle, Amount, Order (Normal, Centre in…), Segments, Parts, element, attribute, family (Intensity first), Runs, Timing: Per cycle | Per head. The plan is docs/plans/fx-editor-plan.md.

The brief

Build stacks, and see what the attributes do

Chris asked for an FX editor in the programmer that helps build stacked FX and shows how an FX's attributes interact, as a timeline of how colour, intensity and the rest change on each head and element. And: what else would make FX programming simpler without taking power away?

One FX at a time

The live editor (FxEditor) opens one FX in a tray, a popover or a busk row. Nothing shows the FX on a selection together, or what they add up to.

No door for a second FX of a family

The FX press takes the selected heads off every other Local FX of its family. Two Intensity FX on one head come only from a palette layer, a Look, a cue or a script.

Local FX sit under the layers' FX

Local FX take the bottom of the programmer band; a layer's FX take the band plus its rank. A Local Pulse loses to a pressed palette's FX, though a Local value beats every layer and the Stack table draws Local on top.

Beat and wall-clock FX do not combine

Each tick loop resets its keys and applies its own FX in its own transaction. A Sine Wave with a Candle Flicker Within it shows one or the other, whatever the blend; the engineering doc says otherwise.

Order is creation order

Two Local FX on one key apply in the order they were made, and nothing can change it. A cue keeps its FX's order; a Look stores none.

Nothing samples an FX off the stage

FX are Kotlin scripts, user FX included, and the client never computes an output. The graph has to come from the desk, through the engine's own functions, which today mutate the instance as they read it.

The editor

List · graph · inspector, on the selection

The FX tab gains Selection · All. All is today's table. Selection is drawn below at desk width, Hex 1–8 selected, three lanes stacked. It works: pick a card, move a control, drag a Local card, press + FX, Solo. The graph here is drawn by a small copy of the desk's rules (spread offsets ported from lib/spreadShape.ts, waveforms from the .fx.kts files) so the page can run on its own; the editor will draw the desk's samples.

How the attributes meet

From Order to the stage, in the order the desk applies them

The graph's marks and the ladder are this pipeline drawn. Steps 1–5 decide when each element's cycle starts; 6–8 turn the clock into a phase; 9–11 turn the phase into what reaches the stage.

1 · OrderEach element gets a rank

Normal, Reverse, Centre out, Centre in, Random, Ping-pong, Position, Unified.

2 · SegmentsNeighbours share a rank

Segments 2: elements 1–2 move together, then 3–4.

3 · WingsThe ranks mirror

Wings 2: the second half runs the first half backwards. Not under a mirrored Order.

4 · PartsThe ranks repeat

Parts 2: the spread repeats every two elements, so odd and even alternate.

5 · SpreadRanks become offsets

100 % spreads the ranks over one cycle; 50 % over half; 0 % is unison.

6 · Cycle · TimingThe clock

Cycle is one pass, on a master or in seconds. Per head stretches it by the ranks.

7 · Phase · RunsEach element's own clock

The clock less its offset, plus Phase. Outside Runs an element holds what is underneath.

8 · DirectionForward, back, bounce

Read on the element's own cycles.

9 · ShapeThe waveform

The FX's parameters: levels, colours, sizes, ratios.

10 · BlendOver what is underneath

Replace, Within, Add, Max, Min; Around for movement. Folded in apply order.

11 · AmountHow much reaches the stage

before + (blended − before) × Amount × FX amount

Building a stack

Two FX on one attribute is a choice, not an accident

Today a second FX of a family always replaces the first on those heads. The editor keeps that as Replace and adds Stack: the new FX goes on top and nothing comes off. The list makes the order visible and changeable, and Record keeps it.

Int

Intensity stacks Within

A Dimmer Strobe stacked on a Sine Wave runs inside it: b × e / 255. The chase still reads; the strobe rides it.

Pos

Position stacks Around

A Circle stacked on a Pan Sweep orbits the sweep: b + e − 128.

Col

Colour and Beam stack Replace at 50 %

Half of the new FX over the old one. Raise Amount to take over, lower it to tint.

Ord

Drag to reorder; Record keeps it

A cue's FX already store an order; a Look's gain one. Local FX move above every layer's FX, as Local values sit above layers.

Simpler, same power

What else the editor can do for the operator

Each is a faster way to set fields that already exist. None stores anything new, and every field stays reachable.

D11

Shapes

Nine named spreads, each with its drawing: Unison, Chase, Back, Centre out, Centre in, Pairs, Odd/even, Mirror, Random. One tap sets Order, Spread, Wings, Segments and Parts; the lit chip is the one the fields match, else Custom.

D9

The offset ladder and hover lights

Five dot rows, one per spread step, so Segments, Wings and Parts can be read before they are run. Hover a control and the graph lights what it changes.

D12

Handles on the graph

Drag a cycle's edge for Cycle, the chase line for Spread, a row sideways for Phase, the level lines for min and max. Each writes the field the inspector writes, through the same 50 ms floor.

D13

Match

Same cycle as… and Same spread as… another FX in the list, copied once, so a colour chase and an intensity chase can be lined up in one move.

D14

Solo in the graph

See one FX alone over what is underneath without touching the stage.

D6

Replace · Stack

The family rule stays the default; stacking is one segment away and starts on a blend that makes sense for the family.

4.4

Tempo on the toolbar

The master's BPM and Tap where the cycle is being read.

—

Left out, on purpose

A waveform drawn by hand, per-element overrides inside one FX, Amount over 100 % and live links between FX were declined in earlier plans or are follow-ups (FU-FX-AMPLITUDE, FU-FX-LINKED-TIMING). Drawing the graph's moment on the Stage view is FU-FX-STAGE-SCRUB.

Other places

The same graph wherever an FX is edited

Phone, or a narrow window. The list folds to lane chips, the graph to a strip of the picked FX's lane, the inspector below. No handles under a finger.

The FX palette editor. The strip under its fields, on eight notional heads or the selection, replaces today's striped preview.

All. Today's table, every running FX. A row's press selects that FX's heads and opens it in Selection.

The fixture sheet's tray keeps its rows and gains ⤢, which opens the programmer's FX editor on that fixture. The cue editor's FX tab and the busk Effects tab draw the strip under the open row.

On the desk

Three changes behind the views

A preview route, sampled by the engine

POST /fx/preview takes the selection, running FX by id or drafts, and a window; it answers each element's samples per attribute, Result, each FX's own output, the start offsets, holding spans, the ladder, and the clock for the playhead. It calls the engine's own phase, spread, waveform, blend and weight functions, split out of the instance so reading them changes nothing, and a parity test holds it to the tick. Stateful FX are sampled from a fresh, seeded copy and marked representative.

One compose pass

Either clock wakes one pass that resets each covered key once and folds every FX, beat and wall-clock, in apply order, in one transaction. Mixed stacks then mean what the graph shows. Closes FU-PERF-FRAME-TXN-UNIFY.

Apply order and Stack

Local FX move above layers' FX in the band and get a rank; programmer.moveFx changes it. The press takes stack: true. A Look's FX gain sort_order, synced at formatVersion 39.

Questions

Fifteen calls, each with a recommendation

Answer by number; the recommended answer is first.

Q1

Where does the editor live?

The FX tab's Selection view.

Or a fourth rail tab (too narrow for a graph); or a route of its own (loses row C and the selection).

Q2

Beat and wall-clock FX on one key?

Fix the engine: one compose pass.

Or keep two loops, warn in the list and draw the graph as the stage behaves.

Q3

A second FX of a family on the same heads?

Ask Replace · Stack, starting on Replace.

Or always stack and let × take the old one off; or a modifier key on the press.

Q4

The blend a stacked FX starts on?

Intensity Within, Position Around, Colour and Beam Replace at 50 %.

Or the FX's own starting blend, which puts a second Intensity FX on Replace.

Q5

Apply order editable and kept?

Yes; Look FX gain an order, v39.

Or creation order, drawn but fixed.

Q6

Local FX above layers' FX?

Yes, as Local values are.

Or keep today's ranks and draw them.

Q7

Where does the graph's data come from?

The desk's sampler.

Or a client copy of the waveforms, which misses user FX and Steps and doubles every rule.

Q8

What is underneath in the graph?

What the attribute holds now.

Or a flat zero; or a level the operator picks.

Q9

Rows in which order?

The picked FX's Order, with Rig to hold them.

Or always rig order.

Q10

Handles in the first version?

Yes: Cycle, Spread, Phase, min and max.

Or the inspector only, handles later.

Q11

Shapes?

Yes, the nine.

Or none; or a list the operator saves.

Q12

Match?

Copy once.

Or a stored link that follows later edits.

Q13

Solo?

The graph's only.

Or a stage mute, which needs a flag beside Amount.

Q14

Other hosts?

Palette editor, cue FX tab and busk Effects tab get the strip; the tray links in.

Or the programmer only.

Q15

Names?

FX editor; list, graph, inspector; Shapes; offset ladder; Match.

Timeline is the stack view's mode, so the graph is not called that.
