Setting up a surface without dragging

The current Surfaces view has three problems. Making a binding means dragging from a library that doesn't reach everything. A fixture, a group and the selection can each do things the others can't. And controls disagree about which value they read back. This page proposes one grammar for every binding and one rule for where it applies, then four ways to edit them. You can click and change things in each of the four mockups.

Research · the current view and the code behind it

What's wrong today

One card for each of your three points, with what the code does. References are to frontend/src and src/main/kotlin/…/lighting7.

The drag library is the only quick way in, and it doesn't reach everything
  • For tempo, the library offers master 1 only, fixed at 60–180 BPM. Any other master or range means drag, then Change target, then a form: about ten gestures. SurfaceLibrary.tsx:683
  • Flash, fire trigger, grand-master level, set tempo and pick up pad exist only in the form. A fixture flash can't be made there: the flash starts on a group attribute with no way to switch it. BindingTargetPicker.tsx:1270
  • MIDI Learn works only on a control that already has a binding, and then it copies that binding to whichever control you move. SurfaceInspector.tsx:227
  • An encoder's turn and its push share one binding, so a GO dropped on an encoder replaces what the turn did.
  • Below tablet width there is no picture and no library, and the table there can't see strip bindings. A strip's fader reads "unbound", and its + creates an override.
  • The A/B buttons in the header switch the hardware's layer, so looking at layer B changes the desk.
The selection can do things a fixture or group can't, and the other way round
  • Selection only: Locate, Fan, FX speed, Press palette, Press pad. No binding locates or fans a named group. midi/BindingTarget.kt
  • Named only: Flash (the selection can't flash), Fire trigger (a fixture, not a group) and Strip (no strip follows the selection).
  • The encoder bank reaches only strip encoders. No selection encoder follows it. StripDerivation.kt:29
  • Missing everywhere: Clear, Blind, Release a target. Undo is the only programmer-wide verb.
  • Press-only, no explicit on or off: Locate, Press palette, Press pad. Cue stack Pause only cancels the follow; nothing resumes it. fx/CueStackManager.kt:724
  • "Bank" means two things on one screen: the device's A/B layers, and the encoder bank.
Every control writes the programmer, but they read back different things, and pan and tilt use the wrong key
  • A level, colour or pan control writes the programmer, but reads back the stage: the composed value, before the grand master. So pickup arms against a cue's moving value, and in Blind the control keeps showing the stage while it edits the staged programmer. SurfaceFeedbackPublisher.kt:1319
  • The fan moves from the programmer's value, falling back to the stage, and shows its own amount. FX speed shows the lead FX. routes/programmerFan.kt:495
  • Pan and tilt write their own pan / tilt keys. Position FX, palettes and the fan use position, and a pan entry doesn't hold back a position FX. Under a Circle, the write is stored but the stage keeps circling. A dimmer write in the same spot wins. fx/FxSuppression.kt:73 · fx/FxTarget.kt:719
  • The fixture sheet had this exact bug and fixed it by writing setPosition (fixture-fx-sheets D8). The surface never got the fix.
  • There are six different jump and pickup rules. A strip encoder has no jump guard, and pushing it sends the attribute to full.

Patterns to borrow

  • Select the control, by touching it or clicking it, then say what it does.
  • A vocabulary of object × function, filtered by what the control is, with options that don't fit greyed out and the reason given.
  • Scope as an explicit choice: this group, or the selection.
  • Whole-wing setups, edited per control afterwards.
  • Start from the record: assign a cue stack to a button from the stack itself.
  • The picture for "what does this do", a table for "what is everything doing".

Anti-patterns to avoid

  • A drag library across hundreds of targets (today's view).
  • Learn that only works for some control kinds (Lightkey can't learn a fader; today's Learn can't start on an unbound control).
  • Labels that don't come from the binding, or that show ids (Go · stack 3, uuid prefixes).
  • Settings behind hidden gestures (MagicQ's triple-click), and defaults nobody can find (takeover reads Immediate where the desk uses pickup).

Menu names come from search summaries of the vendors' pages. The MA and ChamSys manual sites couldn't be reached from this session, so check exact wording before quoting it in UI copy.

Proposal 1 · independent of which editor wins

One grammar: does, on, how

Every binding answers three questions, and every editor below asks them in the same order:

Does · On · How

Does is the function: Level, Colour, Fan, Flash, Locate, GO, Tap and so on. The list is filtered by what the control is: a fader, an encoder's turn, an encoder's push, or a button.

On is the subject: the selection, a group, a fixture, a record (cue stack, cue, Look, palette, pad, speed master), or nothing for a desk function.

How is the behaviour. A button is Toggle, While held, On or Off. A select is Toggle, hold to replace. A fader or ring picks up or jumps. A binding also says which layer it applies on.

What changes in the model

  • Every fixture function takes all three subjects: the selection, a group, a fixture. Selection attribute, Group attribute and Fixture attribute become one function with a subject.
  • A toggle gets its explicit On and Off forms as a How, rather than a separate target each (Look set, Blackout set, Loose cue set today).
  • An encoder's push is its own binding, separate from its turn.
  • New: Clear, Blind, Release, Record, Update, and Locate, Fan and FX speed on a group or fixture. Fire stays fixture only.
  • The A/B layers keep the name the device prints on them. The encoder bank becomes the encoder page.
✓ works ½ works with a catch (hover) ✕ missing + added by the proposal — doesn't apply

With no subject at all: Undo, Redo and the programmer's Undo exist today. Clear, Blind, Record and Update don't; the agreed model adds all four.

Proposal 2 · the layer a control works on

One layer rule

Moving every control onto the output would break the composition model, and they all write the programmer already. What should change is what they read back, and which key they write.

The rule
A fixture control writes Local on the attribute's own key. It shows, and moves from, what Local holds there, or the cue layer beneath where Local holds nothing.
  • Pan and tilt write position, keeping the other axis, as the fixture sheet does. They then hold back a position FX, as a level holds back a dimmer FX.
  • The readback is Local first. A motor won't chase a programmer FX playing on top of your value, Blind shows the value you're editing, and pickup arms against your value instead of a moving cue. Where Local holds nothing, it shows the cue layer beneath, without FX, so a running FX doesn't drag a motor either. It looks beneath a held flash and ignores park.
  • One jump rule for every fader and ring: a move far from what's shown is ignored until it comes back, as the fan already does.
  • Every binding names its layer in one word: Local Playback Master Tempo Desk. Local is the fixture sheet's word for the programmer's own values.

The alternative is to show the stage everywhere, which is today's rule for sliders. It's simpler, but it keeps the Blind mismatch and the motor chasing a programmer FX. The masters (grand master, blackout) stay where they are: after composition, under park.

Proposal 3 · four editors over that one model

Four ways to edit it

All four edit the same bindings. A, C and D below share one set, so a change in one shows in the others. The sample rig is a small theatre show: eight groups on the strips, palettes on button row 2, encoder pages on row 3, selection wheels on the right.

A

Touch it, then say what it does Session 3

Click a control, or touch it on the desk, and the panel asks Does · On · How for each of its parts. This replaces the library, the edit mode and the form. Try Encoder 11, Strip 3's fader, or the hardware button below.

X-Touch Compact in · out Viewing
Viewing a layer here doesn't switch the desk.

Good at

  • One door reaches every function. Nothing is form-only or drag-only.
  • Touching the hardware finds the control, the way MagicQ, Avolites and Hog do.
  • Works the same at phone width, with the picture swapped for a list.

Costs

  • Setting up 39 buttons is 39 visits.
  • The Does list is long, so it needs grouping and search.

Replaces

  • SurfaceLibrary, edit mode and drag-and-drop.
  • BindingTargetPicker's 40-kind list.
  • Learn as a separate overlay.
B

Give each part of the desk a job Session 5

Pick a job for a whole region, then order its items: eight groups as channel strips, the selection's wheels on the right, palettes on a row. The picture fills in. Per-control changes go through A afterwards. MagicQ's wing modes and grandMA3's executor configurations work this way.

X-Touch Compact A job either stays live or is written once as ordinary bindings (question 2 below).

Good at

  • A useful desk in six choices.
  • Reordering the groups re-points the strips, as a strip binding does today.
  • A job can follow live state: the busk page that's showing, or one head per strip of the selection.

Costs

  • Each device profile needs its regions named.
  • A job kept live has to say what happens when one of its controls is bound by hand: the hand wins, as direct beats strip today.

Builds on

  • Strips are already a job for one column. This widens the idea to whole regions.
C

The mapping sheet Session 3

Every control part as a row on the sheet kit the patch list and the cue sheet already use. Shift-click a run of cells in On, then fill them in order. This sheet reads the same bindings as A, so a change here shows on A's picture.

Good at

  • The whole desk at a glance, on any screen width. It replaces BindingMatrix, which can't see strips.
  • Bulk edits: fill, clear, copy layer A to B.
  • Dead bindings sort and filter like any column.

Costs

  • Doesn't show where a control physically sits.
  • Five columns of choices per row is dense for a first-time setup.

Reuses

  • components/sheet/: the marquee, the batch editor, Clear, and the selection bar.
D

Assign from the thing itself Session 6

Every record that can go on a surface gets Assign to surface… in its menu: cue stacks, cues, Looks, palettes, groups, fixtures, speed masters, pads, busk pages. You press a control on the desk, and the app offers the functions that fit. This is Eos's [Sub] 5 [Load] and grandMA3's Assign, from the object's side.

Good at

  • Starts where the operator already is: the stack card, the speed master sheet, the group.
  • The record shows where it's bound (GO on Button 36 · A). Today only fixtures and groups do.

Costs

  • Needs the desk attached, or a picture to click on instead.
  • One menu item on about nine kinds of record.

Not on its own

  • It can't make a selection binding or a desk function. It's an entry point into A, not a replacement for it.
Plan

Six sessions

The order comes from the code review. The binding and its migration go first, because every later session builds on them. A session that touches both sides of the wire is one PR, as the repo's rules ask.

  1. The binding and its migration. Builds the grammar: one Binding(does, on, how) with a part for an encoder's turn and push, a spec per function, and one dispatch. Pan and tilt write position. One expansion of a subject's heads reaches elements and white-mix colour. Records are named by uuid only. Flash is stored as a percentage.

    Database step 15 and an archive translate, sync formatVersion 53 with minReader 53. OSC moves onto the new grammar with its addresses unchanged. Strip rows carry across as they are. Operators notice three things: a strip encoder's push stops sending full, flash levels change on heads whose range ends below 255, and a pan nudge holds tilt.

  2. Feedback. Builds the layer rule's readback: Local over the cue layer, without FX, looking beneath a held flash and ignoring park, read by a loop off the ArtNet thread. One jump rule for every fader and ring. One entry type, with each function's LED declared on it, including new LEDs for GO, a cue held loose and encoder pushes. Layer, encoder page and held flashes per device.

  3. The served picture, and editors A and C. The desk serves each control's resolved binding and one function catalogue, takes a write by slot, and has an identify mode that captures a touch without firing it. Builds A, the inspector, and C, the mapping table. Viewing a layer stops switching the hardware.

    Removes the library, the target form, BindingMatrix and the Learn overlay (below).

  4. New functions. Clear, Blind, Release, Record (a new cue on a named stack, with the Record sheet's defaults) and Update (refused when nothing is included). Locate, Fan and FX speed on a group or a fixture. Flash and "follow the encoder page" on the selection.

  5. Jobs. Job regions with ordered slots in each device profile, the stored jobs and their sync folder, live derivation, and strip rows turned into jobs with their own database step. Builds B.

  6. Assign from the thing. Assign to surface… on cue stacks, cues, Looks, palettes, groups, fixtures, speed masters, busk pads and busk pages, and each record showing where it's bound. Builds D.

Removed in session 3

  • The drag library, edit mode and their drop rules (SurfaceLibrary, lib/surfaceDrop.ts's drag half).
  • The 40-kind target form. A's panel takes its place everywhere it opens.
  • Learn as an overlay that copies a binding. Touching a control selects it, and the panel's own Learn fills a subject from the screen.
  • The header's bank buttons as a way of viewing. Viewing a layer stops moving the hardware.

Where each part of this design lands

  • The grammar: session 1, with its new functions and subjects in session 4.
  • The layer rule: the position key in session 1, the readback in session 2.
  • A and C: session 3. B: session 5. D: session 6.
Agreed

Decisions

Chris's answers, 8 Nov 2026. The code review measures the surface code against these.

  1. The readback: Local first. A fixture control shows and moves from what Local holds for its attribute, or the stage where Local holds nothing. In Blind that is the staged value.

  2. Jobs stay live. A region's job is stored, as a strip is today. Reorder its items and the controls move with them. A control bound by hand leaves the job.

  3. All four editors, now. A is the editor, B sets up regions, C is the overview and the phone view in place of BindingMatrix, and D puts Assign to surface… on the records. The drag library and the 40-kind form go.

  4. Programmer verbs: Clear, Blind, Release and Record. Release takes the selection, a group or a fixture as its subject.

  5. Record is two functions. Record names a cue stack and appends a new cue with every touched value, all attributes, with nothing to answer. Update writes back to whatever Include loaded, as the programmer's Update does.

  6. An encoder's push is its own binding, and does nothing until one is made.

  7. Flash takes its level as a percentage of each head's range, as a fader scales, and takes the selection as a subject like every other fixture function.

  8. Words: Layer A / B for the device's layers, Encoder page for the encoder bank, and Writes to Local on a fixture binding, matching the fixture sheet.

From the code review

  1. "The stage" in the readback is the cue layer under Local, without FX. A running FX doesn't drag a motor or re-arm pickup; a GO still moves un-held faders as it fades. The readback looks beneath a held flash and ignores park.

  2. Identify mode captures without firing. While the app waits for a touch (A or D), the next press is captured and not dispatched: one press, a banner, a 30 s timeout.

  3. The desk serves each control's resolved binding and one function catalogue. The browser keeps no copies of the backend's rules, and a fixture shared by Kotlin and TypeScript guards the wire.

  4. Bindings per device type, live state per device. Two identical surfaces share bindings, but each keeps its own layer, encoder page and held flashes.

  5. Fire stays fixture only, and minReader 53 is accepted: an older desk refuses a newer show rather than losing its bindings.
