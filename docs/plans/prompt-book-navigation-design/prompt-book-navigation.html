Design · 2026-11-28 · Prompt Book

Finding your place in a long script

The Prompt Book can only be scrolled, or jumped through from one cue to another. This proposes search, bookmarks for acts, scenes and musical numbers, and three smaller aids, so that "the kitchen scene" or "the line about the lamp" is two keystrokes away while a show is being programmed. Claude gets the same reach through the MCP tools.

What the book has today

Read from the code at e03b8e9.

  • Navigation is by cue. A click in the rail scrolls to that cue's anchor; Jump to live and a ?cue= link do the same. A stretch of script with no cues yet, which is every stretch on day one, has no handle at all.
  • Only a window of pages is drawn (about one viewport above and below), because an 84-page script whole is 1.5 GB of canvas. So the browser's own find reaches five or six pages and misses the rest.
  • The desk never reads the script's text. It stores the PDF by hash and serves the bytes. Claude places a cue by estimating a fraction down a page (pdfPage, y) from its own reading of the PDF, and cannot ask the desk where a line is.
  • Stack markers ("Act 1", "Interval") divide a cue stack and show in the rail between cards. They have no place on the script: the anchor routes refuse a marker's entry.
  • The page count and Front matter offset exist, so the desk already knows that PDF page 5 is the script's printed page 3.

A working sketch: type in the find field, press Enter for the next hit, click a heading in the rail, or click and drag the strip between the script and the rail. The script is invented for the drawing.

Search

Find in the whole script

In the view

  • ⌘F (or /) opens the find field in the book's toolbar, in place of the browser's own find, which cannot see undrawn pages.
  • Hits are found as you type, across every page: a count (3 of 14), every hit washed yellow on the page, the current one orange, and a tick per hit on the strip.
  • Enter and ⇧Enter step through them; Esc closes the field and offers Back to where the search began.
  • Case and accents are ignored, and a hit may run over a line break, since a script's line breaks are the typist's. Speaker names and stage directions are text like any other.
  • It also searches cue names, cue notes, script notes and bookmark titles, listed above the script hits, so "blackout" finds the cue called that as well as the stage direction.
  • A scanned page has no text. The field says 12 pages are scans and cannot be searched rather than answering 0 results.

Where the text comes from

  • The desk reads the PDF's text once, when the script is imported or first asked for, and keeps it beside the PDF as a per-page index: each run of text with its box in the same 0–1 page coordinates anchors and notes use.
  • The index is derived from the PDF's bytes and named by its hash. It is this machine's cache: never synced, rebuilt when missing.
  • The browser fetches the index once and searches it locally, so typing is instant and a hit's box is known before its page is drawn.
  • The same index answers Claude (below), which is why the desk owns it rather than the browser's PDF renderer.
  • No OCR. A scanned script is searchable only if the PDF already carries a text layer.
Bookmarks

Acts, scenes and numbers as places on the script

A bookmark is a title at a line of the script, with a kind. It belongs to the book, travels with the show in sync, and needs no cue to exist.

FieldHoldsNotes
TitleThe Kitchen, Lamp-Lighter's WaltzWhat the operator calls it. Up to 120 characters.
KindAct · Scene · Number · OtherSets the colour and the indent. A number is its own track: it can start in one scene and end in the next, so kinds are not a strict tree.
LabelI.3, #7Optional, short: what the script or the score numbers it. Shown on the flag and the strip.
PlacePDF page and a height on itOne point, where it starts. A bookmark's order is its place; there is nothing to reorder. A section runs to the next bookmark of its kind or above.

Placing one

CueRecordNoteCutBookmark
  • Unlocked, select the heading's text: the selection toolbar gains Bookmark. The title is filled from the selected text, and the kind guessed from it (ACT, SCENE, No.), both editable in a small sheet.
  • The tool palette gains a Bookmark tool for a scanned page: click a line, type the title.
  • A bookmark shows as a flag in the left margin, above the cue markers' lane. Unlocked it drags up and down like an anchor; a click opens its sheet (rename, kind, delete).
  • Import the PDF's own outline appears once, on a book with no bookmarks whose PDF has one. Many exported scripts do.
  • Every write is a show-history entry, so ⌘Z takes it back.

Bookmarks and a stack's markers

They overlap: both say "Act 2 starts here". They differ in what they divide. A marker divides one stack's cue list, is ordered by hand, and shows in the Show view and the cue sheet. A bookmark divides the script, is ordered by its place, and exists before any cue does. The proposal keeps them two records and makes the book do the joining:

  • The rail groups cues under bookmark headings, by where each cue is anchored. No marker is needed for the book's rail to read I.2 The Kitchen over Q5–Q9, and the headings stay right when a cue's anchor moves.
  • A stack marker still draws in the rail, except one whose name matches the bookmark heading directly above it, which would say the same thing twice.
  • Add as stack marker on a bookmark's sheet (and for all of a kind from Contents) puts a marker of that title before the first cue anchored after it. It is a copy, made once: renaming either later does not rename the other.
  • A marker never becomes a bookmark by itself. Markers in a show today are untouched.

The alternative, a marker placed on the script is the bookmark, needs no new table, but a bookmark then needs a stack to live in, every scene and number becomes a row in the Show view's cue list, and its order is the stack's rather than the script's. It was declined.

MCP

What Claude gains

Three changes to the existing prompt-book tools and one new tool. Claude still reads the attached PDF itself for meaning; the desk now tells it where things are.

ToolChange
get_prompt_bookAnswers bookmarks (title, kind, label, place, the cues inside), whether the script has text, and how many pages are scans. With pages: [3, 4] it also answers those pages' text, line by line with each line's y.
search_prompt_book new{query, pdfPage?, limit?} answers every hit with its page, its box, the line it is in and the bookmark it falls under. Read-only, any project.
mark_up_prompt_bookTakes bookmarks: upserted by title and kind, so a second run moves a bookmark instead of doubling it; removeBookmarks by title. markers: true also adds the stack markers.
every atA place may be given as a quote: at: {text: "Who lit the lamp?", pdfPage?: 4}. The desk finds the line and anchors to its real box. A quote found nowhere, or in several places with no page to choose by, is refused naming the candidates. This reaches cue anchors and notes in mark_up_prompt_book and build_cue_stack too.
 
// Claude, having read the script, populates the outline in one call
mark_up_prompt_book({
  bookmarks: [
    { kind: "ACT",    title: "Act 1",                 at: { text: "ACT ONE" } },
    { kind: "SCENE",  title: "The Lamp Room", label: "I.1", at: { text: "SCENE 1. The lamp room" } },
    { kind: "NUMBER", title: "Lamp-Lighter's Waltz", label: "#1", at: { text: "(MUSIC: No. 1)", pdfPage: 2 } },
  ],
})
Also proposed

Three smaller aids

Each is small beside search and bookmarks, and each answers a moment that comes up while programming.

AidWhat it doesThe moment it is for
The stripA 22px column between the script and the rail: the whole script top to bottom, with a tick per bookmark, cue, search hit and warn or safety note, the live cue in green and the viewport as a box. Click or drag to move."Where are the cues thin?" and "how far is the interval?", answered without scrolling.
Go to pageThe toolbar's p.34 / 84 is a field, in the script's printed numbering (the front-matter offset applied). G focuses it.The director says "from the top of page 34".
BackAfter any jump (a search hit, a bookmark, a page, a rail click) a chip offers ↩ Back to p.12. One step, replaced by the next jump, gone after a manual scroll past a page.Checking a line elsewhere, then returning to the cue being built.

The follow scroll keeps its rule. Every jump here is the operator's own, so it never waits and it cancels a follow still waiting. With the show running and locked, the next GO onto a new sequence scrolls the book back to the live cue, as it does after a rail click today.

Nine decisions

#Decision
D1The desk extracts the script's text into a per-page index beside the PDF, in page coordinates, keyed by hash. Machine-local and derived, never synced.
D2Search runs in the browser over that index, across script text, cue names and notes, script notes and bookmark titles. It replaces ⌘F in this view.
D3A bookmark is the book's own record: title, kind, optional label, a point on the script. Portable, synced with the show, journaled for undo.
D4The rail's headings are derived from bookmarks and each cue's anchor. Bookmarks and stack markers stay separate records; Add as stack marker is a one-time copy.
D5Contents lives in the toolbar: a breadcrumb that opens a filterable list. No new permanent panel.
D6MCP places things by quoting the script. at.text on every place, search_prompt_book, page text on request, and bookmarks in get_ and mark_up_prompt_book.
D7Navigation is never an edit. Find, Contents, the strip, pages and Back all work locked and for both roles. Placing, moving and deleting a bookmark need the lock off, as an anchor does.
D8No OCR. A scanned page is named as unsearchable; bookmarks and Go to page still work on it.
D9The strip, Go to page and Back, as tabled above. Marking unprogrammed cues (What's left) was declined.

Asked and answered

Chris's answers on 2026-11-28.

QuestionAnswer
How do bookmarks relate to a stack's markers?Two records, joined by the book. A marker placed on the script being the bookmark, and a marker linked live to its bookmark, were declined.
Who reads the script's text?The desk. One index for the view and for Claude. The browser reading it alone was declined.
Where does Contents live?The toolbar's breadcrumb and its list. A tab in the rail and a left panel were declined.
Which of the smaller aids?The strip, Go to page and Back. What's left (a mark and filter for cues with nothing programmed) was declined.

Four sessions

Each one PR, in this order, each useful when it lands.

Session 1

The script's text

The desk's text index and its route; search_prompt_book, page text in get_prompt_book, and at.text on every place. Claude benefits before any UI exists.
Session 2

Find, pages and Back

The find field and its hits on the page, search over cues and notes, the scanned-page message, Go to page, and the Back chip.
Session 3

Bookmarks

The record, sync and undo; placing and editing in the view; flags, the breadcrumb and Contents; the MCP's bookmarks; the PDF outline import.
Session 4

The rail's headings and the strip

Cues grouped under bookmark headings and Add as stack marker; the strip with every tick kind and its drag; the keys that step between bookmarks and between cues.
