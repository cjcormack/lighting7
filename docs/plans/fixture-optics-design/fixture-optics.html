Fixture optics: every beam channel the Stage view can see

The two Source Four Revolutions on the Commemoration Hall's balcony zoom, frame and scroll on the rig but not on screen, and focus can't visibly land on the back wall. This plan fixes them, and the same gaps across the rest of the fixture library.

APPROVED Nine sessions, one PR each Engineering plan: docs/plans/fixture-optics-plan.md Evidence: docs/plans/fixture-optics-design/INDEX.md

Where this page and the engineering plan disagree, the plan wins.

What was reported

Three symptoms, three causes

"The zoom doesn't do anything"

The Stage view turns a zoom channel into a beam angle only when the fixture type declares the angle at each end. The Revolution's declares neither, so the beam stays at the family's fixed 19°. ETC's manual gives 15°–35°.

"I can't focus it on the back wall"

The focus range does reach the wall, which is 24 m away. But the blur model scales with a lens size the Revolution never declares, so every setting from about DMX 141 up draws the same edge on a far wall. Gobos never land on a surface, which is how focus is judged on the rig.

"Frames, scroller and wheels have no effect"

Those channels are typed as generic settings, which the Stage view never reads. No type for a framing shutter exists, and the scroller's frames have no colours attached.

Session 2's vocabulary

Framing shutters as DMX channels

The renderer can already cut a beam with four blades: that is how a conventional lantern's shutters are focused today. What's missing is a way for DMX channels to drive it.

Two new categories close the gap. SHUTTER is a blade's insertion and SHUTTER_ROTATION its angle. Each names the edge it cuts, with its depth at full and its rotation range. Lantern blades and DMX blades then share one shader path. The packing widens from ±30° to ±45° in 1.5° steps, which still fits two blades per float.

ESTIMATE The manual doesn't say which frame is which side, how deep a blade reaches, or which way rotation turns. The mapping drawn here is a guess, marked at its source, and checked on the rig afterwards.

Decisions

  1. D1Optics are facts about the fixture type, declared on its annotations. What is loaded into one unit (gels, gobos, media) is placement data. Neither lives in a Look.
  2. D2A zoom must declare its angles, and a stepped zoom declares one per band. A test enforces both.
  3. D3A fixed-lens head declares its field angle on its type.
  4. D4Framing shutters get their own categories, each naming its blade and declaring its depth and rotation.
  5. D5DMX and lantern blades share one shader path, widened to ±45°.
  6. D6Fitted media is set per unit, defaulted from the type, and synced with the show.
  7. D7The gel library moves to the backend, so templates and MCP can name a fitted gel's colour.
  8. D8A scroll or random colour band animates through the wheel. Nothing draws black for want of data.
  9. D9Focus blur follows the distance from the focal plane, scaled per type, and the edge is properly sharp at the plane.
  10. D10Gobos land on surfaces. Shadows stay a follow-up.
  11. D11Focus here solves the focus value for a point and writes it to the programmer, as aim does.
  12. D12Strobe bands declare what they do. A closed shutter draws dark, and a strobe flashes no faster than three times a second on screen.
  13. D13Resets and lamp control become fixture commands, refused everywhere a property can be stored and run from a confirmed menu.
  14. D14Travel time is drawn, never sent. The Stage view eases toward the DMX value at the fixture's own speed.
  15. D15Every value no manual states is marked as an estimate where it is declared, and checked on the rig.
In order

Nine sessions

Session 0 goes first. Sessions 1 and 2 need only it. Session 4 needs session 3 for the Revolution's gobos, but not for the MAC 250's or Robe's. Sessions 5 to 8 can run in any order.

0

The Revolution, corrected

backend

Zoom angles, lens and body size, the scroller's colours, the forward wheel as a gobo wheel with rotation, the rear wheel removed, fan speed exposed, the media frame in and out. The personality notes are corrected against the manual.

Done when the zoom sweeps the pool between 35° and 15°, and frame 9 draws Full CTB.

1

Focus that reads

frontend · one route

Depth of field recalibrated per type. Locate parks focus at the middle of the range, not mid-DMX. Focus here from the Focus tab, and through aim_fixtures.

Done when a Revolution on the back wall goes soft 3 m either side of the wall, and Focus here lands it sharp.

2

DMX framing shutters

backend + frontend

The two categories, the Revolution's frames mapped, blades read and packed every frame, Locate putting them out and square.

Done when each blade cuts its named side and turns about its own edge.

3

Fitted media

backend + frontend · format 22

The gel library on the backend with the eight missing gels. Each unit's scroller string, wheel slots and media frame, edited from the patch sheet, the Focus tab and MCP.

Done when one unit draws a custom string while the other keeps stock, and both survive sync and a clone.

4

Gobos on surfaces, stacked wheels

frontend

Gobos projected onto walls and floors, blurred by focus. A second gobo wheel multiplied over the first, and the Robe's second colour wheel. Measured in Safari and on an iPad; it falls back to the selected heads if the budget runs short.

Done when a MAC 250 gobo lands on the back wall and blurs as focus moves.

5

The library optics pass

backend + frontend

Fixed-lens angles, the Robe's stepped zoom and proportional bands, Varytec previews, animated no-colour bands, the Robe's pan/tilt, the Whex fixes, and the fixture docs rewritten with a beam vocabulary section.

Done when a Varytec beam is never black, and the Robe zooms in three steps.

6

Strobe and closed shutters

backend + frontend

Every strobe band declares closed, open, strobe with a rate, random or pulse. The Stage view draws each, within the three-flash rule.

Done when a MAC 250 at strobe 0 is dark on screen as on the rig, and a 20 Hz strobe draws a shimmer.

7

Fixture commands

backend + frontend

Resets and lamp control move out of properties and options into commands, refused by name wherever a property is stored. A confirmed Commands menu; the desk holds the band for its time. MCP and the public listener are refused unless an admin allows them.

Done when no Look, template, cue or programmer path can hold a reset, and Reset scroller holds for 3 s and drops.

8

Travel time

backend + frontend

Each type's base speeds, and the Revolution's own timing channels at one second per step. The Stage view eases what it draws; DMX output is untouched.

Done when a five-second colour time scrolls from frame 1 to frame 9 through the frames between.

Checked on the rig afterwards
  • Which module is in each unit's front bay, and what it holds. Until then the iris and the forward wheel both stay live.
  • Whether the scroller carries ETC's stock string.
  • Which way zoom, iris and focus run.
  • Which frame is which side, how deep a blade reaches, which way rotation turns, and whether 128 is square.
  • Where the media frame's wings go in.
  • The wheel's index range and rotation direction.
  • Pre- or post-2007 hardware: the manual covers the earlier units only.

Left out on purpose
  • The Revolution's 14, 15 and 23-channel modes.
  • Custom gobo artwork; fitted gobos use the desk's 16 patterns.
  • Shadow maps.
  • Dragging blades on the pool itself.
  • Lasers, and haze that follows the hazer.
  • LED and movement programs drawn as canned animations.
  • Strobe as a rate in templates.
