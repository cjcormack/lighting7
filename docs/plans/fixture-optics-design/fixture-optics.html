Fixture optics: every beam channel the Stage view can see

The two Source Four Revolutions on the Commemoration Hall's balcony zoom, frame and scroll on the rig but not on screen, and focus can't visibly land on the back wall. This plan fixes them, and the same gaps across the rest of the fixture library, in nine sessions.

The brief, 2026-10-02

Three complaints about one fixture

"The zoom doesn't do anything"

The iris does work, which rules out a wiring fault. The zoom channel reaches the Stage view and has no effect there.

"I can't focus it on the back wall"

The focus range was lengthened once already, to 40 m. The operator still can't find a setting that lands sharp on the wall.

"Frames, scroller and wheels have no effect"

Four framing blades, a 14-frame gel scroller, two beam wheels and a media frame. Nothing moves on screen.

Chris asked for the problem to be widened into a survey of every fixture type, expecting new features to be needed. Three surveys ran in parallel: ETC's 48-page manual, every type definition in the library, and every channel the Stage view decodes.

Project 15, as stored on the desk

A 24-metre throw onto an 8.6-metre wall

Diagnosis 1

The zoom channel has nothing to convert

The Stage view resolves a beam angle from the first of these that has one:

  1. the zoom channel, through its declared degMin / degMax;
  2. the patch's beamAngleDeg, a static override;
  3. the lantern's field at its focused zoom (generic dimmers only);
  4. the family's default field: mover:profile is 19°.

The Revolution's zoom declares no angles, so step 1 returns nothing and the beam falls through to step 4. The code's own comment names the Revolution as a known case (FixtureModel.tsx:1165). The Robe ColorSpot is the other.

The fix is one annotation, degMin = 35, degMax = 15, plus a test that fails any ZOOM slider without angles, so the next type can't repeat it.

Diagnosis 2

The focus curve reaches the wall; the renderer hides it

The proposed sharp window is DMX 243–249. At 21 m the wall reads 0.77 hard, at 27 m 0.85, at 24 m fully sharp. Six DMX steps is realistic and hard to find by hand, which is why session 1 pairs the recalibration with Focus here: the desk solves the DMX value for the point the beam lands on. Gobos landing on the wall (session 4) give the eye something to focus on.

Across the library, today

  • No strobe channel is drawn. A closed band is dark on the rig and lit on screen.
  • No timing or speed channel is modelled: the view snaps to every value.
  • No type declares a lens diameter, and none can declare a fixed field angle.
  • A colour option without a preview draws the beam black, including every scroll and random band.
  • Five types expose a reset as an ordinary option a Look, cue, template or effect can hold.
  • The beam vocabulary is undocumented: categories, gobo, prismFacets and colourPreview live only in KDoc.

Left as they are

  • The laser: no laser rendering exists, and none is planned.
  • Hazer and fog output: FU-STAGE-HAZE-FOLLOWS-HAZER.
  • LED and movement programs: drawing a guessed program is worse than drawing none.
  • The Slender Quad's heads tilting together: FU-STAGE-INDEPENDENT-HEADS.
  • Philips Hue: there are no Hue fixture types to survey.
Design

The data path, and what each session adds to it

Every optic travels the same route: an annotation on the Kotlin fixture class, reflected into a descriptor, serialised to the client, found by category, decoded by the director every frame, and drawn by two shaders. Most of the plan is new vocabulary flowing down this path; nothing about it changes shape.

Blades: one shader path for lanterns and DMX heads

Lantern shutters already cut the beam in both shaders. They are packed once per body spec and written into the light table every frame. Session 2 reads the eight DMX channels instead, every frame, into the same scratch packing. A unit has either lantern blades or DMX blades, never both.

  • Locate takes blades out and rotation to square.
  • Channels join the director's beamChannels, so a blade move asks for a frame.
  • The packing test changes with the step.

Fitted media: what a unit has loaded

A type declares which settings are loadable and what is in them from the factory. A placement overrides any option by name. Two units in one rig can carry different strings, so this lives beside each placement's lantern focus, not on the type, and travels with the show in sync.

  1. The placement's fitted content for that option;
  2. else the type's stock content;
  3. else, for a band with no single colour, the wheel's own previews animated (D8).

The gel library moves from frontend/src/data/gels.ts to a backend resource served at GET /api/rest/gels, because the template resolver's wheel snap and describe_rig both need a fitted gel's colour. Fitted gobos are picked from the desk's 16 patterns; custom artwork is a follow-up.

Fixture commands: resets out of reach of a Look

Five types offer a reset as an ordinary setting option today, selectable from a Look, cue, template, the programmer or an effect. The MAC 250's and Robe's resets are script methods. Session 7 makes them all commands, a kind modelled on one-shot triggers: outside composition, refused by name everywhere a property can be stored.

  • Refused at: Look and template rows, cue assignments, the programmer, effects, Record, busk pads, surface bindings and MCP property tools (COMMAND_NOT_STORABLE). Raw channel writes are dropped, as a trigger's are.
  • Run from: the fixture panel's Commands menu, behind a confirm naming the unit and the command.
  • Remote: the public listener and MCP are refused unless an admin allows them, like effects.
  • Busy: a second command to a unit mid-hold is 409 COMMAND_BUSY.
  • Stored rows naming a removed RESET option are stripped once at startup, logged.

Strobe and closed shutters

Every strobe band declares what it does. The view draws CLOSED dark and OPEN lit. STROBE flashes at its band's rate. RANDOM and PULSE run on the same clock, from elapsed time passed into the director, so the test harness stays reproducible.

The Stage view runs on screens other people watch. WCAG 2.3.1 allows no more than three flashes a second, so a faster strobe draws as a reduced-contrast shimmer. The DMX is untouched.

The Hz annotation is the data half of FU-TMPL-STROBE-HZ. A Hertz intent in the template grammar stays there.

Travel time: drawn, never sent

The Stage view snaps to every DMX value today. A real head takes time: its motors have a top speed, and the Revolution's own timing channels stretch a move to one second per DMX step. Session 8 keeps a displayed value per family in the director and moves it toward the DMX value at the allowed rate.

It only changes what is drawn. The desk never second-guesses what a fixture does with its own timing.

Few manuals publish motor speeds, so most base speeds are estimates. The Revolution's timing scale is in the manual.

Decisions and their reasons

D1Optics are fixture-type facts on the annotations. What is loaded into a unit is placement data. Neither lives in a Look.The manual settles the bands; the venue settles what is in the slots.
D2A ZOOM must declare its angles; a stepped zoom declares one per band. A test enforces both.A zoom without angles is silently inert, which is how the Revolution's went unnoticed.
D3A fixed-lens head declares fieldDeg on its type.The Fusion, IMG Wash and Scantastic all document theirs, and today only a per-patch override can say so.
D4SHUTTER and SHUTTER_ROTATION categories, each naming its blade, depth and range.Frame numbers say nothing about sides; the renderer needs sides.
D5One shader path for lantern and DMX blades, widened to ±45° in 1.5° steps.Two blades per float is the packing's budget; finer than 1.5° is finer than anyone focuses a frame.
D6Fitted media per placement, defaulted from the type.Two units can carry different strings. Follows the stage-view plan's D14 for focus data.
D7The gel library moves to a backend resource.The wheel snap and describe_rig are backend, and both need a fitted gel's colour.
D8No-colour bands animate; every other COLOUR option must declare a preview.Black is the one answer certain to be wrong. Chris chose animation over open white.
D9Relative blur with a per-type gain; full hardness at the focal plane.The desk will never know apertures well; a tuned constant is honest about that and shows a focal plane.
D10Gobos land on surfaces. Shadows stay a follow-up.The gobo is what anyone focuses by. Chris chose the full treatment; the budget check guards it.
D11Focus here solves the DMX for a point and writes the programmer, as aim does.The sharp window at 24 m is six DMX steps. A solve finds it; a hand rarely does.
D12Strobe bands declare their kind and rate; the view flashes within the three-flash rule.The Stage view is on screens other people watch.
D13Resets and lamp control become fixture commands, refused wherever a property is stored.A reset in a Look is a show-stopper one click away on five types. Triggers already prove the pattern.
D14Travel time is drawn, never output.The fixture owns its timing; the view only shows it.
D15Every unstated value is marked // Estimate: and checked on the rig.Chris chose to build on estimates and correct afterwards.
P1Nine sessions, one PR each.The repo's git workflow: a branch and a PR per change.
P2One formatVersion bump, 22, for fitted media.An older reader would drop it and push the loss back. Everything else is type data.
P3The front bay stays both: iris and wheel live.Nobody knows which TCH's units hold.
P4Project 15's fitted media is Chris's to set on the desk.It is show data, not a migration.

Risks and limits

What could go wrong, and what is left out

Scope honesty

  • Most Revolution numbers are estimates. Sixteen values rest on ChamSys, defaults or judgement.
  • The manual may be the wrong one. It covers pre-May-2007 units.
  • One of the iris and the wheel is dead on the real unit, until the front bay is known.
  • Depth of field is tuned by eye.
  • Gobos on surfaces have an unmeasured cost; the fallback narrows them to the selection.
  • Travel speeds are mostly guesses.

Migration

  • No DB migration: media is additive.
  • Session 7's strip of stored RESET options runs once at startup, logged.
  • Project 15's fitted media is Chris's to set.

Out of scope

  • The Revolution's 14, 15 and 23-channel modes.
  • Custom gobo artwork.
  • Shadow maps (FU-STAGE-QUALITY-TIER's other half).
  • Dragging blades on the pool (FU-STAGE-FOCUS-ON-POOL).
  • Lasers; haze that follows the hazer.
  • LED and movement programs as canned animations.
  • A Hertz intent in templates.

Follow-ups to record as sessions land

  • FU-STAGE-PROGRAM-MACROS (session 5)
